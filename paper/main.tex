\begin{abstract}
Statistical postprocessing of ensemble forecasts is usually trained on
multi-year, per-station archives; a zero-shot time series foundation
model needs none. Using EUPPBench \EuppbenchVersion{}'s German 2\,m
temperature subset (\NStations{} stations, 0--\MaxLeadHours{}~h; all
methods scored on \NMatchedInstances{} matched instances), we
compare a frozen TimesFM-3 (ensemble mean as a past-future covariate)
against Ensemble Model Output Statistics (EMOS) and a one-parameter
variance-inflation rescaling. At full training data, every trained
method beats TimesFM-3 on the Continuous Ranked Probability Score
(CRPS); so does a fixed-climatological rescaling using no training data
at all. Pooled EMOS's CRPS crosses TimesFM-3's at \BpCrpsPooledCases{}
cases (\BpCrpsPooledDays{} days), though the crossing is significant
only from \BpCrpsPooledSigK{} cases (\BpCrpsPooledSigDays{} days)
onward, for both EMOS variants, under day-blocking.
80\%-nominal coverage crosses more slowly (\CoverageCrpsRatioPooled{}x
and \CoverageCrpsRatioLocal{}x later than CRPS, pooled and local EMOS).
TimesFM-3 retains a calibration-sharpness advantage: even a
leakage-optimistic multiplier fit on test-set coverage needs
\InflLeakyWidthDiffPoint{} K more at matched coverage (95\% CI
[\InflLeakyWidthDiffCiLow{}, \InflLeakyWidthDiffCiHigh{}]). Its
short-lead CRPS advantage reverses by \DurableCrossoverHours{}~h; a
persistence-augmented EMOS closes only \PersistenceGapClosedPct{}\% of
the gap, which does not support an information-asymmetry
explanation, though not from architecture. Supplying ensemble spread as
a second covariate does not close the calibration gap either: it
worsens CRPS and reaches nominal coverage only with a wider interval.
Overall, on CRPS this
frozen model is mostly not competitive with a few days of
station-specific training data -- with one exception that survives even
a leakage-optimistic bound, and a short-lead advantage that the
information-asymmetry explanation does not account for.
\end{abstract}
